PearPlay

Your browser’s video. Your Apple TV.

PearPlay sends compatible web videos from Chrome directly to Apple TV—without screen mirroring, re-encoding, or a cloud relay. Choose a video, find your TV, and send it to the bigger screen.

Browser video on your TV, without sharing your desktop

Send the stream, not your screen

Your Apple TV plays the compatible video from its source. PearPlay does not mirror your desktop, re-encode the video, or relay it through a cloud service.

Choose what plays and where

Start the website’s video, find available videos and TVs, and choose your Apple TV. Pair if asked, then select Send to TV. Pairing never sends a video automatically.

Browser controls before casting

Play, pause, seek, jump, mute, or use Picture in Picture for the selected browser video before casting. These controls operate in your browser—not on the TV. During supported TV sessions, PearPlay shows pause/resume and read-only progress. The active cast survives popup reopening and tab switching. Browser mute is deliberate, never automatic; preferred TV identity is selected after rediscovery without saving media addresses.

Local setup, no extra account

Install the helper on your computer and connect your browser once. Pairing stays with your OS user. Mac, Windows and Linux have experimental downloads with different compatibility limits.

How PearPlay sends video to Apple TV

PearPlay sends a compatible media URL to the receiver. The Apple TV fetches it from the site. The browser extension and local helper carry the controls—not the video.

Send a video in three steps

One-time setup comes first: load the extension and install the local helper. The first-run setup tab checks the connection; reopen it with Setup in the popup.

  1. Access

    Allow, then find.

    Grant website access on a click, start the video, then choose Find videos. This version requires all-sites access for discovery.

  2. Destination

    Choose the video and TV.

    Connect the helper and find Apple TVs on your network. Select the stream and receiver; pair if asked.

  3. Send

    Send. Watch. Confirm.

    Press Send to TV. Check for video and sound on the TV. If the browser is also playing, use Mute browser video to silence only the original browser video, not the TV.

Frequently asked questions

Is PearPlay screen mirroring?

No. PearPlay hands a compatible video URL to the Apple TV, which fetches the stream directly. Your desktop stays on your computer. The TV must be able to access the video without your browser’s cookies.

Do I need the PearPlay Helper?

Yes, for TV playback. Install the helper for your computer, open PearPlay Setup, connect your browser, and fully quit and reopen it. The Chrome extension alone does not provide the connection to Apple TV.

Can I pause or seek on the TV?

Pause TV and Resume TV require helper 0.3.1 or newer and a supported authenticated Apple TV session. Read-only TV progress needs helper 0.3.3; TV seeking, jumps and volume are unsupported; use the TV remote. End helper session closes the helper connection and does not guarantee the TV stops.

Will it work with every player?

No. The receiver needs a directly fetchable HTTP(S) media URL. A blob: URL cannot be handed off. PearPlay does not transfer browser cookies, bypass DRM or geography, or defeat access controls. Signed and expired URLs may fail. Use only media you are authorized to access.

Can I allow just one website?

The popup offers a per-site grant, but Find videos currently stays disabled without all-sites access. That limitation is disclosed rather than presented as working site-only discovery. Access is requested on a click, not silently at install.

Does it block ads?

No. PearPlay respects normal ad delivery and does not bypass DRM, geography or access controls. A pre-roll-to-program transition was observed on one tested stream; that does not establish compatibility with every service.

Privacy: local control, no PearPlay cloud

The extension communicates with a helper on your machine. Pairing credentials stay in user-local storage: restricted file permissions on Linux and Mac, with Windows protection implemented using DPAPI and access controls. Pairings are not synced between computers. The media origin and Apple TV still communicate over the network; “local” does not make the stream anonymous. No telemetry endpoint is present in the audited extension/helper sources.
