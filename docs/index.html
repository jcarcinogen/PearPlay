The stream takes
the direct route.

PearPlay sends a compatible media URL to the receiver. The Apple TV fetches it from the site. The browser extension and local helper carry the controls—not the video.

Media sourceThe site’s streamReceiver-fetchable HTTP(S)
→
ReceiverYour Apple TVNo PearPlay media relay

Your desktop stays on your desktop.

No screen mirroring, re-encoding, or extra computer relaying the video. PearPlay passes the source URL; it does not promise a particular resolution or site compatibility.

Local by design.

No PearPlay account or cloud service required. Pairing credentials stay on your machine. You choose what to send and where it goes.

A deliberate handoff.
Three choices.

One-time setup comes first: load the extension and install the local helper. The first-run setup tab checks the connection; reopen it with Setup in the popup.

  1. 01 / Access

    Allow, then find.

    Grant website access on a click, start the video, then choose Find videos. This version requires all-sites access for discovery.

  2. 02 / Destination

    Choose the video and TV.

    Connect the helper and find Apple TVs on your network. Select the stream and receiver; pair if asked.

  3. 03 / Send

    Send. Watch. Confirm.

    Press Send to TV. Check for video and sound on the TV. If the browser is also playing, stop it with the website’s controls.

Before you send.

Useful limits, not small print.

Will it work with every player?

No. The receiver needs a directly fetchable HTTP(S) media URL. A blob: URL cannot be handed off. PearPlay does not transfer browser cookies, bypass DRM or geography, or defeat access controls. Signed and expired URLs may fail. Use only media you are authorized to access.

Can I allow just one website?

The popup offers a per-site grant, but Find videos currently stays disabled without all-sites access. That limitation is disclosed rather than presented as working site-only discovery. Access is requested on a click, not silently at install.

Does it block ads?

No. PearPlay respects normal ad delivery and does not bypass DRM, geography or access controls. A pre-roll-to-program transition was observed on one tested stream; that does not establish compatibility with every service.

Private control.
No extra account.

The extension communicates with a helper on your machine. Pairing credentials stay in user-local storage: restricted file permissions on Linux and Mac, with Windows protection implemented using DPAPI and access controls. Pairings are not synced between computers. The media origin and Apple TV still communicate over the network; “local” does not make the stream anonymous. No telemetry endpoint is present in the audited extension/helper sources.
